The project titled "Modular Cargo Loading Optimization Software" is 4-person team project that was part of UC Riverside's Mechanical Engineering Capstone project for HexHomes.

## My Contributions (Jerry045)
As a member of the four-person development team, I contributed to the optimization logic, trailer constraints, panel arrangement, testing, and application interface throughout the development of MCLOS both in Alpha and Beta Versions.

### Optimization & Arrangement
- Contributed to the panel arrangement and optimization logic, including changes to arrangement behavior, input handling, and stress-related calculations.
- Developed and tested alternative arrangement approaches intended to improve packing efficiency and accommodate different panel orientations.
- Worked on vertical panel orientation and testing, including development of multiple test versions for vertical placement.
- Contributed to later-stage improvements that enabled six-pod configurations to fit on the 53-foot trailer and restored support for the 42-foot trailer with vertical panel orientation option.
- Contributed to adding a greedy placement algorithm and separating axis theorem (SAT) collision for rectangular and trapezoidal panels.

### Trailer & Geometric Constraints
- Implemented and tested trailer spacing/gap configurations and contributed to trailer-specific loading behavior.
- Added a 14-foot height constraint for flatbed configurations to enforce the project's transportation height requirement.
- Worked on roof-panel geometry and arrangement testing, including simplified roof shapes and alternative placement approaches.

### Application & Visualization
- Added dark/light mode functionality and contributed to the application's visual presentation and branding (Later Removed).
- Integrated the HexHomes logo and related interface/theme changes.
- Contributed to iterative testing and refinement of the Streamlit application throughout the Alpha and Beta stages.

### Development & Testing
- Maintained and tested multiple development versions of MCLOS while evaluating changes to panel placement, orientation, trailer configurations, and arrangement strategies.
